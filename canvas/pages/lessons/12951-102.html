3.10 Human Behaviour Influences

Syllabus

Explore by implementation how patterns in human behaviour influence ML and AI software development, including:

  • psychological responses
  • patterns related to acute stress response
  • cultural protocols
  • belief systems

  Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention - Students will:

  • Explore how patterns in human behaviour, including psychological responses, acute stress reactions, cultural protocols, and belief systems, influence the development of machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) software.

Success Criteria - Students can:

  • Understand how psychological responses and stress-related patterns shape ML and AI system design.
  • Analyse how cultural and belief systems impact AI development.
  • Explore how these behavioural factors are incorporated into AI algorithms and systems through case studies and practical examples.

  Content

Human behaviour plays a significant role in shaping the development of machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) systems. AI developers and researchers aim to create algorithms that not only analyse data but also interact effectively with users and reflect human behavioural patterns. Understanding how people respond psychologically, how stress affects decision-making, and how culture and belief systems influence behaviour is critical in designing AI systems that are ethical, user-friendly, and contextually appropriate.

 

Psychological Responses

Humans react emotionally and psychologically to technology, and these responses shape how AI systems are designed to interact with users. Factors like trust, fear, and cognitive biases are crucial considerations when building AI systems that users rely on.

Key Concepts:

  • Trust and User Adoption:

    • Users are more likely to adopt AI systems if they trust the recommendations or decisions made by the system. Trust is built through transparency (e.g., explainable AI) and reliability.

  • Fear and Resistance:

    • Psychological fear of AI, often referred to as "automation anxiety", can affect how users interact with AI systems. Developers need to create systems that feel intuitive and friendly to reduce resistance.

  • Cognitive Bias:

    • AI systems must be designed to account for human cognitive biases. For example, users may experience confirmation bias, where they favor AI outputs that confirm their pre-existing beliefs.

Example in Implementation:

In healthcare AI, systems are designed to provide transparency in their decision-making processes to build trust with medical professionals. When suggesting a diagnosis, AI systems often provide explanations for their recommendations, easing concerns that doctors may have about handing over too much control to a machine.

 

Acute Stress Response

Acute stress can significantly influence how humans interact with AI systems, especially in high-pressure environments such as emergency services or military operations. People under stress may have impaired decision-making abilities, rely more on heuristics, and exhibit fight-or-flight responses, which must be considered when designing AI that operates in these contexts.

Key Concepts:

  • Fight-or-Flight Response:
    • In stressful situations, users may need quick, simple solutions rather than complex, data-heavy recommendations. AI systems should be designed to offer straightforward, easily digestible information during critical moments.
  • Cognitive Load:
    • High-stress environments increase cognitive load—the amount of mental effort required to process information. AI systems must minimize the cognitive load on users by providing clear and concise outputs, avoiding overwhelming amounts of data.

Example in Implementation:

In AI-driven emergency response systems, like those used in disaster management, stress patterns are considered by presenting critical information in a simplified format. Visual alerts, concise instructions, and real-time updates are prioritised to help first responders make quick, informed decisions under pressure.

Cultural Protocols

Cultural diversity influences how people perceive and interact with AI systems. Developers need to consider cultural norms and practices when designing AI systems for different regions or communities to ensure the technology is respectful, acceptable, and useful across various cultural contexts.

Key Concepts:

  • Cultural Sensitivity:

    • AI systems should be tailored to align with the cultural practices and expectations of the target users. This can include language preferences, gender roles, or traditional values that differ across cultures.

  • Localisation:

    • Localisation of AI refers to adapting the system to meet the specific cultural, linguistic, and social needs of different populations. This includes language translation, cultural references, and contextually appropriate interactions.

Example in Implementation:

Voice assistants like Amazon Alexa or Google Assistant often undergo significant cultural adaptation when being deployed in different countries. For instance, these systems are programmed to recognise local dialects, respect local customs (e.g., addressing people more formally in certain cultures), and follow region-specific social norms.

 

Belief Systems

Belief systems, including religion, moral philosophies, and ethical frameworks, shape how AI systems are perceived and accepted in various societies. Developers must account for these belief systems to avoid ethical conflicts and ensure that AI systems operate within acceptable moral boundaries.

Key Concepts:

  • Ethical AI:

    • Developers must ensure that AI systems do not conflict with the ethical and moral standards of their users. For instance, certain communities may oppose AI in areas like surveillance or genetic modification based on religious beliefs.

  • Bias and Fairness:

    • AI systems must be designed to avoid bias, especially in culturally diverse societies. Developers should incorporate fairness and inclusivity into AI algorithms, ensuring that systems do not discriminate based on gender, race, religion, or other belief systems.

Example in Implementation:

In facial recognition technology, ethical AI development considers potential biases against specific racial or ethnic groups. Developers implement fairness techniques to ensure that the system works equally well across different demographics, reducing the risk of unfair treatment.
