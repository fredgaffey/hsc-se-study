Unit3 Mastering the HSC Exam (Short Answer)

  Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention - Students will:

  • Learn how to answer questions accurately and with the right level of detail for the number of marks.
  • Learn how to structure short answer responses based on the NESA key verb being used.

Success Criteria - Students can:

  • Apply their knowledge to answer short answer questions.
  • Structure their responses based on the NESA key verb.
  • Produce responses of the appropriate length for the question type and number of marks.

  Content

How to Answer "Short Answer" Questions

Procedure:

  1. Read the question carefully to identify the NESA key verb and topic area.
  2. Analyse any provided stimulus.
  3. Plan your response quickly in your head based on the NESA key verb and mark allocation.
  4. Write your response. Ensure you relate to the scenario/include examples.
  5. Re-read the question and make sure your answer meets the demands of the question.

 

Different Types of Short Answer Questions

Short answer questions can range in difficulty, expected length, and marks. Normally the difficultly of the key verb defines how many marks and how long your response needs to be. For example an identify question is normally 1-2 marks, and far easier to answer than an explain question which is normally 3-4 marks.

NESA Key Verbs You Need to Know

  • The NESA key verbs you are likely to encounter in your HSC Enterprise Computing HSC exam are listed below.
  • We will be looking at an example of each on this page so you can get an idea of how to structure your response since the key verb directly dictates the structure and content of your response. Without the correct structure you may not be able to get full marks, so it's important you understand and practice responses to different key verbs. 
  • You may sometimes find that two key verbs have been used in a single question. For example a recommend and justify question is a fusion of two higher end verbs which requires you to provides reasons in favour of why each recommendation answers the question. These fusion questions are often worth more marks and more structurally and cognitively complex.
Verb Band Description
Identify Band 1–3 Recognise and name.
Outline Band 1–3 Sketch in general terms; indicate the main features of.
Describe Band 2–4 Provide characteristics and features.
Compare Band 3–5 Show how things are similar or different.
Discuss Band 3–5 Identify issues and provide points for and/or against.
Recommend Band 3–5 Provide reasons in favour.

Explain

Why/How

 Band 4–6
  • Relate cause and effect.
  • Make the relationships between things evident.
  • Provide why and/or how.
Justify Band 4-6 Support an argument or conclusion.
Assess Band 5-6 Make a judgement of value, quality, outcomes, results or size.
Analyse Band 5-6
  • Identify components and the relationship between them.
  • Draw out and relate implications.

 

Common Mark Allocations & Expected Time Spent and Response Length

  • In the exam, you get roughly 1.8 minutes per mark, however you want to working slightly ahead of this if you can to ensure you have time for harder questions. This means you have about 5 minutes to complete a 3 mark question and about 9 minutes to complete a 5 mark question, etc. 
  • In the online exam environment there will be a word counter below of your answer text box and it will show something like "0/150 words", which shows you how many words you have written and how many words you are expected to write. Note that you are able to write more than the recommended number, there is no word limit. The word indicator will just start to show something like "183/150 words".
  • If you have the knowledge and time to write a little extra to include an "insurance policy" point, this is often a good idea so long as you stay within the time allocated for the question. However, it's not a good idea to write a lot less than the amount unless you are very succinct. A correctly structured response at the length specified is a recipe to get full marks.
Marks Max Time Allocation (1.8 mins per mark)  Time to aim for
1-2 1.8–3.6 mins 1-3 mins
3-4 5.4–7.2 mins 5-7 mins
5-6 9–10.8 mins 8-10 mins
7-8 12.6–14.4 mins 12-14 mins

Note that you should be aiming to complete question in slightly less time that in the table since some harder questions may require more time so you want to have a little time to spare at the end so you can go back to questions you flagged and develop more complex answers further. Use this table as a guide for the maximum time to spend on each question, but don't hesitate to work faster as long as you are confident. It's also important not to rush. Give each question it's due attention and love.

How to Get Full Marks in Short Answer Questions

  • Relate to the scenario and give examples. If there is a provided stimulus, you must engage with it by responding to ideas/requirements it imposes. If there is no stimulus, you still need to come back to the terms of the question and use examples which relate to the topic and requirements of the question. You are aiming to write an answer that is tailor made for the question and wouldn't fit any other. This shows that you've truly understood the question and will make it easier for the marker to pay you marks.
  • Structure your response appropriately. Make sure you structure your response appropriately for the NESA verb and number of marks. Each verb has a very specific type of response it wants you to engage in and if you don't follow this structure you can loose one or more marks. The examples below will take you through how to structure your response for each of the most common NESA verbs.
  • Know your content — You need to be on top of your content so that you can add accurate detail to the body of your answers. Structuring the response is the first and most important thing, but then the actual substance of the response that sits in the frame of the NESA verb will come from your understanding of the course content. Your examples/relation to the scenario will be added in addition to your content recall to bolster your response and prove you actually understand it.

Question: Outline the difference between artificial intelligence and machine learning. (2 marks)

Step 1. Read the Question Carefully.

When reading a question, it's important to ask these two questions

  1. What is the topic/content area? This question is testing your knowledge of machine learning and artificial intelligence. Specifically it's testing the dot point "Distinguish between artificial intelligence (AI) and ML" in your software automation topic. Revise 3.2 Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning, if required
  2. What are we required to do? Highlight the NESA key verb in red so you don't forget it. We are required to outline the difference between artificial intelligence and machine learning. This means we need to sketch in general terms what distinguishes them.

There is no stimulus for this question so we will skip step 2.

Step 3. Plan your response quickly in your head.

Since this is a 2 mark outline question we don't need too much content. We will start by outlining what AI is and then outline what machine learning is and how it differs from AI.

Step 4. Write your response

  • For an outline you just need to sketch in general terms each of your points so we will only write a sentence or two for each point.
  • Relate to the Scenario/Question. Even though this is a simple question we will need to adhere to the golden rule "relate to the scenario/question". This means you should use terms from the question throughout your response. Below, the links back to the question have been highlighted in purple. 
  • Give clear examples. When there is no explicit scenario, it's important to give examples to enhance the clarity and nuance of your answer. Since this is an outline question, you don't need to go into too much detail.

Sample Answer Analysis: Artificial intelligence is the technology or concept that involves machines simulating human intelligence to perform analog, variable, reasoning, decision-making, and problem-solving tasks. Machine learning is the process by which artificial intelligence is created and trained. It focuses on training algorithms to allow computers to learn patterns from data and improve their performance without explicit programming. AI is the end result of machine learning.

Step 5. Check your response is correct

Quickly reread the question and try highlighting your response in your head if you're in an exam or in a word processor if you are practicing. Make sure there is enough purple and blue and also some green and yellow.

  1. We have sketched both artificial intelligence and machine learning in general terms and how they differ.

Marking Criteria (through the eyes of a marker)

How do I get full marks?

  • Structure your response appropriately. To get full marks you just need to outline the difference between artificial intelligence and machine learning.
  • Know your content — You need to be on top of your ML and AI content. Revise 3.2 Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning.

I'm completely stuck. How do I maximise my marks?

  • Don't leave the question blank. If you're totally stuck, there are always some basic things you can do to guarantee at least one or two marks. After this, flag the question so you can come back to it after you finish the rest of the exam, hopefully with fresh ideas to claim a few more marks.
  • As per the marking criteria, if you don't know how to outline both and show the difference, you could also just identify a difference without outlining it or outlining what machine learning or artificial intelligence is without their differences. If you're about to run out of time, you can also just write something you think is relevant and it may end up being correct.

Explain

Explain Questions

NESA Definition — Explain

  • Relate cause and effect.
  • Make the relationships between things evident.
  • Provide why and/or how.

The explain definition from NESA requires us to relate cause and effect. By relating cause and effect, we are able to make the relationship between things evident and provide how something works or why it works.

WHY/HOW Questions

You may also find that some questions in the HSC don't use a specific NESA key verb, but rather use "Why" or "How". When you see these words, you can basically treat them loosely as an "Explain" question. A why question will require you to come up with reasons explaining/linking to a specific outcome and how requires you to relate how components of something work together/what makes something work.

 

Answer Structure

  1. Brief topic sentence or phrase that addresses the question.
  2. Include one or a series of causes which lead to the effect you are trying to explain. You are aiming to take a complex idea an split it into it's atomic components and map out how each piece relates and contributes to the effect you are explaining.

Developers and enterprises must balance innovation with user empowerment, implementing features like data anonymisation and encryption. For businesses, there is a moral obligation to safeguard customer data and avoid practices that exploit user information for profit. Ethical dilemmas also arise when companies rely on proprietary systems to maintain market advantage, potentially limiting access to secure software for public benefit.

Legally, secure software development involves navigating complex regulatory landscapes. Individuals are protected by laws such as the GDPR, which grant rights over their data and ensure accountability from organisations. For enterprises, compliance with legal standards is both a necessity and a challenge, as breaches can result in substantial fines and reputational damage. Intellectual property laws further complicate the development process, as companies must protect their innovations while respecting the contributions of others. For example, a company may patent its encryption methods to secure its competitive edge while licensing them for collaborative use.

In conclusion, safe and secure software development has profound social, ethical, and legal ramifications, affecting individuals and enterprises in interconnected ways.
