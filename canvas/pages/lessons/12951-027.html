4.1 Requirements

Syllabus

Define and analyse the requirements of a problem, including:

  • demonstrating need(s) or opportunities
  • assessing scheduling and financial feasibility
  • generating requirements including functionality and performance
  • defining data structures and data types
  • defining boundaries

  Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention - Students will:

  • Understand the process of defining and analysing the requirements of a software problem, including identifying needs, assessing feasibility, specifying requirements, and setting clear boundaries for a successful project.

Success Criteria - Students can:

  • Identify the needs or opportunities for a software project.
  • Assess the scheduling and financial feasibility of a project.
  • Define functional and performance requirements clearly and accurately.
  • Specify appropriate data structures and data types.
  • Establish clear system boundaries to avoid scope creep.

  Content

The process of defining and analysing the requirements of a problem is a critical first step in any structured software development process. This initial phase is crucial because it directly influences the success or failure of the project, determining whether the final product will meet the client’s needs and expectations. It is during this stage that developers and system analysts work closely with stakeholders to establish a clear understanding of the problem, identify opportunities for improvement, and lay out the groundwork for design and development.

 

What are requirements?

Requirements are the specific features or functions a system must have to solve a problem or meet its purpose. They provide clear, measurable goals to guide development and ensure the system meets user needs and can be tested for success.

 

Demonstrating Needs or Opportunities

The first step in defining the requirements is to clearly establish the need for the software solution or identify an opportunity where technology can bring improvement. In a real-world context, this involves conducting a feasibility study or engaging with stakeholders (such as end-users, clients, or business owners) to understand their goals and challenges. A software system must have a clear purpose—whether it's solving a specific problem, improving an existing process, or seizing an opportunity for innovation.

You would begin by identifying and documenting the needs through techniques like:

  • Interviews with stakeholders to understand their needs.
  • Questionnaires to gather input from a wide range of users.
  • Observations of current systems or processes to pinpoint inefficiencies.

By the end of this stage, you should have a clear understanding of why the project is necessary.

Scheduling and Feasibility

Before committing to the design and development phases, it's essential to assess whether the project is feasible in terms of both scheduling and financial constraints. Failure to properly evaluate these factors can lead to project delays, overspending, or even abandonment.

  • Scheduling feasibility involves estimating the duration of the project and ensuring it can be completed within a given timeframe. Tools like Gantt charts are commonly used to break down the project into tasks, allocate time to each, and identify potential bottlenecks.
  • Financial feasibility looks at whether the project can be completed within the available budget. This involves estimating costs for resources, personnel, technology, and ongoing maintenance.

By assessing both scheduling and financial feasibility upfront, the development team ensures that the project remains within scope and can be delivered on time and within budget.

Generating Requirements

Once the need is established and feasibility is confirmed, the next step is to generate specific requirements in terms of functionality and performance.

  • Functional requirements describe the specific actions the system must perform. For example, a booking system might need to allow users to search for available appointments, make bookings, and receive confirmation emails. Each function must be precisely defined so that the development team knows exactly what to implement.
  • Performance requirements specify how well the system should perform these functions. This could include:
    • Speed (e.g., "The system must process user inputs within 2 seconds.")
    • Reliability (e.g., "The system must have a 99.9% uptime.")
    • Scalability (e.g., "The system must support up to 1,000 concurrent users without degradation.")

These requirements should be documented in a software requirements specification (SRS), which will act as a reference throughout the development process.

Data Structures and Data Types

As part of the requirements definition process, it is important to define the data structures and data types that the system will use. These elements are essential for efficient data management and processing.

  • Data structures refer to the way data is organised and stored within the system, such as arrays, lists, stacks, queues, or trees. The choice of data structure affects how quickly and efficiently data can be accessed and modified.
  • Data types define the kind of data that will be processed by the system, such as integers, floating-point numbers, strings, or complex objects. Each data type needs to be carefully selected to match the specific needs of the system.

For example, in an inventory management system, you would define a data structure for storing product details, including fields for product names (strings), prices (floats), and quantities (integers). Correctly defining data structures and types is critical for system performance and reliability.

Defining Boundaries

Defining the boundaries of a system is a crucial step in controlling the scope of the project. Boundaries determine what the system will do (in scope) and what it will not do (out of scope). This helps ensure the development team stays focused on the core objectives of the project, avoiding unnecessary features or requirements that could lead to scope creep.

Scope creep occurs when additional features or functionalities are added to a project without proper planning, which can lead to delays, increased costs, and confusion. For example, if you're developing a food delivery app, the core functionality might include order placement, payment, and tracking. However, if you start adding features like social media integration without a clear reason, this could derail the project and stretch resources.

By defining clear system boundaries, stakeholders and the development team remain aligned, ensuring that the project delivers the required solution without exceeding time or budget constraints.

 

Importance of Requirements Phase

One of the leading causes of project failure in software development is the lack of clear and well-defined requirements. Studies show that a significant percentage of software projects fail because they run over budget, miss deadlines, or don’t deliver what users need. Many of these issues stem from poor initial planning and an incomplete understanding of the problem.

To avoid this, the requirements phase must be thorough and accurate. It serves several key purposes:

  • Guiding development: The requirements act as a roadmap for the development team.
  • Validating success: The final product can be tested against the requirements to determine whether it meets its objectives.
  • Setting expectations: Clear requirements ensure that all stakeholders understand what the system will and will not deliver.

 

Requirements Verification

Once the requirements are defined, they must be verified to ensure they are clear, complete, and achievable. Verification involves reviewing the requirements with stakeholders to confirm that they align with the project’s goals and constraints. This step helps to identify any conflicting or unclear requirements before the project progresses to the design phase.

Effective requirement verification also ensures that every requirement is testable. For each requirement, there should be a clear way to measure whether the system meets the specified criteria.
