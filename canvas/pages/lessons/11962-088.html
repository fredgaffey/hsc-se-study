How to Answer SQL Questions

Procedure:

  1. Read the question carefully.
  2. Analyse any provided stimulus.
  3. Create and test your SQL query.
  4. Re-read the question and make sure your answer is correct.

Sample Answer

Question Breakdown & Tips and Tricks

Question: Write a SQL query that displays the contents of the Tasks table showing the taskName, status, dueDate and subjectName in ascending order of the dueDate. Only tasks that are due before 11-03-25 and are not done should be displayed. (3 marks)

Step 1. Read the question carefully

When reading the question, it's important to ask these two questions:

  • What is the topic/content area? Obviously this is an SQL question, but specifically it's testing your ability to select fields from multiple tables, how to use the WHERE clause on two fields and also ORDER the output. When practicing, you should always try to identify where the content is located in your syllabus so you can become more familiar with it and how it informs the questions you are asked. SQL is located throughout your programming for the web topic and specific information on the exact things you are required to know are on page 22 & 23 of your course specifications.
  • What are we required to do? We need to write an SQL query that shows the taskName, status, dueDate and subjectName which you should notice requires selecting data from multiple table. Is also specifies the ordering to be ascending by dueDate. The where clause will need two conditions so that only tasks that are due before 11-03-25 and not done are displayed.

Stimulus:

Step 2. Analyse the stimulus

In this question we would be glancing over to the stimulus as we read the question since the question contains most of the directive information we need to write the query. The stimulus is here only to show us the the layout of the database so we know what fields are in what table as we write each of the clauses in the query.

Before you move on, it might be useful for you to highlight all the parts of the query so you don't forget anything. You have three highlighter colours in the online HSC exam, so you could highlight the fields you need to SELECT in yellow, then you can highlight your conditions for the WHERE clause in red and the ORDER BY requirements in blue.

Step 3. Create and test your SQL query

Writing your Query: There are some simple steps you can follow while writing your query which will help ensure you don't miss anything important:

  1. Always start writing your SQL query by writing the SELECT clause by including the required fields you want to see in the output. At the same time, you should include the tables your fields come from in the FROM clause. Make sure you don't miss any by looking at your yellow highlights.
  2. Next, you should ensure you include all the required conditions in your WHERE clause by looking at your red highlights. These are red because they are important to get right. You should read them carefully to ensure you use the right logical operators. There's nothing worse than being off by one because you misread "tasks due before 11-03-25" as "tasks due up to 11-03-25" and used "<=" instead of "<". 
  3. Finally, you should complete your ORDER BY clause by looking at your blue highlights.

Testing your Query: To test your query, you can click the "Run" button which will be available in your exam environment. It will produce an output which you can inspect to ensure all the correct fields have been displayed and all rows satisfy the WHERE condition(s) and ORDER BY clause. If there is a syntax error in your query, you will be presented with an error that will show you where the error is at the very least and depending on the error even the type/cause of the error.

Step 4. Check your response is correct

For this question, we will quickly glance at our highlights again and ensure the output if the query is correct. You shouldn't need to check too much since after testing your query, it should be in working condition and correct.

 Marking Guidelines (from the perspective of a marker)

How do I get full marks?

  • To get full marks, you need to provide a completely correct SQL query. Ways you can ensure you get full marks in these types of questions are coming up with a criteria for the types of records you would expect to see in the output of your query. If you see records that aren't supposed to be there, or can see ones in the table that haven't appeared in the query output, you know the query must be wrong. Refine it until the output is perfect and when it is, you know you have a correct answer.
  • You need to be on top of your SQL query writing skills. To refresh your understanding and skills, revise 2.18 Web-Based Databases.

 I'm completely stuck. How do I maximise my marks?

  • Don't leave the question blank. If you're totally stuck, there are always some basic things you can do to guarantee at least one or two marks. After this, flag the question so you can come back to it after you finish the rest of the exam, hopefully with fresh ideas to claim a few more marks.
  •  You'll notice in the marking criteria that there is one mark for showing "some understanding of the problem". This means just writing the SELECT clause or maybe ORDER BY clause could get you one mark. This is why we don't leave a question blank since one mark is better than 0. To get two marks you need to provide a "substantially correct" query which means even if you make a few mistakes you could get two marks so long as you have both tables in your FROM clause. Don't give up and with a good attempt, you could get most of the marks.
