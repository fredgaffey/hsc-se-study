3.5 Software

Syllabus
Experiment with software to control interactions and dependencies within mechatronic systems including:
motion constraints
degrees of freedom
combination of subsystems
combination of sensors, actuators and end effectors to create viable subsystems

  Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention - Students will:

  • be able to experiment with software to control interactions and dependencies within mechatronic systems.

Success Criteria - Students can:

  • demonstrate the ability to control motion constraints and understand their importance in mechatronic systems.
  • explain and manipulate the degrees of freedom in various components of mechatronic systems.
  • combine different subsystems effectively to achieve desired outcomes in mechatronic projects.
  • integrate sensors, actuators, and end effectors to create and control viable subsystems, providing examples of their applications.
  • evaluate the performance of their mechatronic systems through experimentation and provide insights on improvements and optimisations.

  Content

Mechatronic systems combine mechanical, electrical, and software components to perform complex, automated tasks. To design and control these systems effectively, software tools play a critical role in managing the interactions and dependencies between components.

 

Motion Constraints

Motion constraints refer to the limits placed on the movement of mechanical components within the system. Software can manage these constraints by:

  1. Kinematic Analysis and Planning: Algorithms calculate the permissible range of movements for each joint or actuator to prevent collisions or exceeding design limits.
  2. Real-time Monitoring and Feedback: Sensors provide real-time data on the position, speed, and acceleration of moving parts. The software processes this data to ensure the components stay within the predefined motion constraints.
  3. Error Correction: If the system detects a deviation from the allowed motion, the software can make real-time adjustments to correct the trajectory or halt the system to prevent damage.

Degrees of Freedom

Degrees of freedom (DOF) describe the number of independent movements a system can perform. Software manages DOF by:

  1. Control Algorithms: Implementing control algorithms like PID (Proportional-Integral-Derivative) to manage the movement in each degree of freedom precisely.
  2. Inverse Kinematics: Calculating the necessary joint movements to achieve a desired position and orientation of the end effector.
  3. Path Planning: Developing trajectories that the system should follow, considering all degrees of freedom to achieve smooth and efficient movements.

Combination Subsystems

Combining various subsystems into a coherent mechatronic system involves:

  1. Integration Middleware: Software that provides a common platform for communication between different subsystems, ensuring they work together seamlessly.
  2. Hierarchical Control Systems: Structuring control systems in a hierarchy where high-level software manages overall system behaviour while lower-level software handles specific subsystems.
  3. Simulation and Testing: Using software tools to simulate the integrated subsystems, allowing for testing and validation before actual deployment.

Combination of Components

Creating viable subsystems with sensors, actuators, and end effectors involves:

  1. Sensor Fusion: Integrating data from multiple sensors to provide a comprehensive understanding of the system’s state and environment.
  2. Actuator Control: Software algorithms translate high-level commands into precise actions by the actuators, ensuring accurate movement and force application.
  3. Coordination: Synchronising the actions of sensors, actuators, and end effectors to perform complex tasks. This involves real-time data processing and control logic to ensure cohesive operation.
  4. Feedback Loops: Establishing closed-loop control systems where sensors provide feedback to adjust the actions of actuators in real-time, maintaining desired performance and compensating for disturbances.

 

Example Scenario: Robotic Arm

Consider a robotic arm with multiple joints (degrees of freedom), equipped with sensors for position and force, and end effectors for handling objects:

  1. Motion Constraints: Software ensures the arm doesn’t extend beyond its physical limits or collide with other objects.
  2. Degrees of Freedom: Control algorithms manage the arm’s joints to move in 3D space, ensuring smooth and accurate positioning.
  3. Combination of Subsystems: The arm’s base, joints, and gripper work together, coordinated by software that integrates motion control, sensor data, and end effector commands.
  4. Combination of Sensors, Actuators, and End Effectors: Sensors provide feedback on the arm’s position and the force applied by the gripper. The software processes this data to adjust the actuators, ensuring the gripper picks up and moves objects as intended.

3.6 Mechatronic Components

Syllabus
Determine power, battery and material requirements for components of a mechatronic system

  Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention - Students will:

  • be able to determine the power, battery, and material requirements for components of a mechatronic system.

Success Criteria - Students can:

  • calculate the power requirements for various components within a mechatronic system.
  • assess and select appropriate battery types and capacities to meet the power needs of the system.
  • identify suitable materials for different components, considering factors like durability, weight, and cost.
  • provide examples of how power, battery, and material choices impact the performance and efficiency of a mechatronic system.
  • justify their choices of power sources, battery types, and materials based on the specific requirements and constraints of the mechatronic project.

  Content

In mechatronic systems, ensuring that each component functions efficiently and reliably requires careful consideration of its power, battery, and material requirements. These systems combine mechanical, electrical, and control elements, each with unique energy demands and operational stresses.

To achieve optimal performance, it is essential to:

  • Determine power requirements for motors, sensors, controllers, and other components.
  • Calculate battery specifications to meet energy needs and ensure uninterrupted operation.
  • Select appropriate materials that provide strength, durability, and thermal or electrical efficiency.

 

Microcontroller/Processor

The microcontroller or processor is the brain of the mechatronic system. It performs all the computations and controls the other components.

    •    Power Consumption: Typically ranges from 0.5 mW to 500 mW, depending on complexity and processing power.
    •    Voltage: Common operating voltages are 3.3V or 5V.
    •    Current: Ranges from a few microamps in sleep mode to tens of milliamps during operation.
    •    Power Management: Sleep modes and power-efficient coding can reduce power consumption.

Sensors

Sensors detect various physical parameters such as temperature, pressure, light, and motion.

    •    Power Consumption: Varies widely, from a few microwatts to several watts.
    •    Voltage: Typically between 1.8V and 5V.
    •    Current: Ranges from microamps to hundreds of milliamps.
    •    Types: Simple sensors like temperature sensors consume less power compared to complex ones like LiDAR.

Actuators

Actuators are responsible for moving or controlling a mechanism or system.

    •    Power Consumption: Can range from a few watts to several hundred watts.
    •    Voltage: Commonly 6V, 12V, or 24V.
    •    Current: Can range from hundreds of milliamps to several amps.
    •    Types: DC motors, stepper motors, and servo motors have varying power needs based on their load and application.

Communication Modules

Communication modules enable the mechatronic system to interact with other devices or networks.

    •    Power Consumption: Typically between 50 mW to 1W.
    •    Voltage: Common operating voltages are 3.3V or 5V.
    •    Current: Ranges from a few milliamps to several hundred milliamps.
    •    Types: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and Zigbee modules have different power profiles.

3.7 Wiring Diagrams

Syllabus
Develop wiring diagrams for a mechatronic system, considering data and power supply requirements

  Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention - Students will:

  • develop wiring diagrams for a mechatronic system, considering data and power supply requirements

Success Criteria - Students can:

  • identify the components required for wiring a mechatronic system
  • explain the data and power supply requirements for the system
  • design wiring diagrams that integrate both data and power supply elements
  • demonstrate the correct connections for data and power supply in a wiring diagram

  Content

From Course Specifications

“If a wiring diagram requires other electrical components, the components must be clearly labelled for identification”

 

Usage of electrical components

Capacitors

Capacitors

  • Used to store energy
  • Decouples components and bypasses power supply voltage drops
    • Removes voltage ripples and suppresses high frequency noise in power supply
    • If voltage drops, capacitor can supply power at correct voltage
  • Filters diode rectified AC signals into a DC-like signal
  • Filters signals of specific frequencies

 

  • How data relates to each other and moves through a system
  • How energy is managed in a system

Diodes

Diodes

  • Acts as a one-way switch for current
  • Rectifies AC signals (removes negative signal) to turn into DC with capacitors
  • Protects reverse current
  • Suppress voltage spikes

Resistors

Resistors

  • Adds resistance to a circuit
  • Limits current for components that need small current and increases voltage for components that need more voltage
  • Used as a pull-up resistor which biases pins to either HIGH or LOW
    • Also prevents shorts with circuits that connect GND to VCC

 

From: https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/pull-up-resistors

 

To wire a microcontroller, the GND pin of each component should be connected to a common ground point, which is typically the microcontroller’s GND pin. For most sensors, to drive the component the VCC pin of the microcontroller would be enough to power, however for motor drivers they would usually require more voltage, therefore depending on the component we may instead wire a separate power supply to the component. Keep in mind the ground must be common, therefore the supply’s ground is also wired to the microcontroller by having a single point where the ground of the separate power supply is wired to the ground of the microcontroller.

3.8 Design and Function

Syllabus
Determine specialist requirements that influence the design and functions of mechatronic systems designed for people with disability

  Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention - Students will:

  • develop an understanding of specialist requirements that influence the design and functions of mechatronic systems designed for people with disability

Success Criteria - Students can:

  • identify specialist requirements for people with disability
  • explain how these requirements impact the design of mechatronic systems
  • analyse the functions of mechatronic systems tailored for people with disability
  • evaluate the effectiveness of mechatronic systems in meeting the needs of people with disability
  • demonstrate an understanding of inclusive design principles in mechatronic systems

  Content

Mechatronic systems are used to support people, including enhancing the quality of life of those with disability. Often this will mean requirements will be made from problems that the system aims to solve or mitigate which are caused by the disability. For example, for users who are wheelchair bound, they would require a way to move around without assistance therefore their wheelchair would need a way to take in input from their users hands, be it with a touchscreen, a steering wheel, or lever, and have that drive the movement in the wheels of the wheelchair which would need to be attached to motors to drive movement.

Mechatronic systems designed for people without disability should also have considerations for those with disability to allow as many people to utilise it. A common case would be designing textual information with not only text, but also braille and also aural communication.
